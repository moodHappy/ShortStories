The Pirate’s Price

海盗的价

Three months later, the lights of the Beaumont ballroom had long gone out. The red ink in the shipping ledgers ran deeper than the autumn. Her mother pressed Margaret’s hand against her own knee. Her father stood at the window and would not turn, saying only that if the ship went down, no one would be left to salvage its dignity.

三个月后，博蒙特宴会厅的灯火早已熄灭。航运账册上的红字比秋天更深。母亲把玛格丽特的手按在自己膝上。父亲站在窗边不肯回头，只说若船沉了，便再没有人能捞起那点体面。

Margaret went to Crowe’s townhouse. The marble floor was cold to the bone. She knelt on the carpet of his study, her silk skirt spreading around her like the gown she had refused to stain with his champagne three months before. Crowe sat in his chair, the same calculating eyes, though he no longer carried a glass.

玛格丽特去了克罗城里的宅邸。大理石地面凉得透骨。她跪在书房的地毯上，丝绸裙摆铺开，像三个月前那件她不肯沾他香槟的长裙。克罗坐在椅中，眼神依旧算计，只是手里不再端着杯子。

“The storm has not passed, Miss Margaret,” he said.

“风暴还没过去，玛格丽特小姐，”他说。

“I know.” Her voice was still steady. Her knees were not. “I have come to beg.”

“我知道。”她的声音仍稳，膝却已经弯了。“我是来求的。”

He did not offer her his hand to rise. He lifted her chin, so she had to look up. “My doors are open to those willing to compromise. The price is not small. I can take your family’s debts. I will take you as well. Not as a mistress. As a concubine. The eighteenth.”

他没有伸手扶她起来，只托起她的下巴，让她不得不仰视。“我的门向愿意妥协的人开着。代价不小。你们家的债，我可以接。你本人，我也要。不是情人，是姨太太。排第十八。”

She closed her eyes, then opened them. “The ship may sink. Let the people live first.”

她闭了闭眼，又睁开。“船可以沉。人先活着。”

The papers were signed that night. The cargo lists at the port changed their heading. The creditors withdrew. Crowe’s hand came to their aid, heavy as another set of chains.

文书当夜签下。港口的货单改了抬头，债主退了门。克罗伸出援手，沉重得像另一副枷锁。

The next year she was nineteen. There were no guests at the wedding, only a concubine’s contract and a side chamber. On the wedding night the candles were still burning. She was still untried. She knelt before her husband, not knowing where to put her hands. Crowe said nothing. He undid his belt, pushed his trousers to his knees, seized the back of her head, and drew her to his crotch.

转年她十九岁。婚礼没有宾客，只有一纸侧室文书和一间偏厅。新婚夜，烛火未灭。她仍是未经人事的身子，跪在丈夫面前，手不知该放何处。克罗二话不说，解开腰带，裤子褪到膝弯，一把攥住她的后脑，将她拉到胯下。

“Open.”

“张嘴。”

She opened. He pressed her head forward and told her to swallow deeper. The head met the back of her throat. She choked. Heat flooded her eyes at once. He did not withdraw. His thighs closed around her head, the inner muscle against her ears, holding her there. Her breath broke into pieces. Blood threaded the whites of her eyes. Tears and spit ran together down his skin. He moved slowly in her throat, each stroke driven to a depth that left her almost voiceless. Her hands braced on his calves, knuckles white, and did not push him away.

她张了嘴。他按着她的头往里送，要她吞得更深。顶端抵到喉口，她呛了一下，眼眶立刻发热。他不退。双腿夹住她的头，大腿内侧贴着她的耳朵，把她固定在那里。呼吸断成一截一截。血丝爬满眼白，泪和唾液一起往下淌，沾湿他的皮肤。他在她喉咙里慢慢抽送，每一记都顶到她几乎发不出声的深度。她的手撑在他小腿上，指节发白，却没有推开。

That was her wedding night. Before she was opened, she was held at his hips and taken down the throat until her eyes were webbed with red.

那就是她的新婚夜。花尚未开，她先被按在他胯下，深喉到眼睛里爬满血丝。

At last he loosened his legs. She sat back on her heels, coughing, a silver thread at her lip, her chest heaving. 他终于松开腿。她跪坐回去，咳嗽，唇边拉着银丝，胸口剧烈起伏。克罗把她从地毯上捞起，按在床沿，裙摆掀到腰上。手指先探进去。她又干又紧。他低笑一声，把唾沫抹在指上，一点点撑开她。她咬住自己的手腕，没有叫出声。他不急着进去，只用指节刮弄那一点，直到她腿根发抖，不由自主地往他手上送。然后他才扶着自己，一寸寸顶开那层初次的阻力。痛意让她弓起背。他按住她的腰，进到最深处，停着等她适应，再开始又深又慢的撞击。床柱轻轻碰墙。他俯在她耳边说，船现在是他的了，人也是。

Outside was the night of 1999. Crowe’s hand rested on the nape of her neck. It was not heavy. He did not take it away.

窗外是1999年的夜。克罗的手搭在她后颈上，并不重，却没有拿开。
