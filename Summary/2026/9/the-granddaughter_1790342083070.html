She sat on the edge of her childhood bed in the guest room, the old print resting on her knee, and stared at the young man who had never grown old. Thomas, 1944. Her grandmother’s words kept circling.

The next Sunday she returned. And the Sunday after that.

Eleanor did not always remember her name, but she always remembered the photograph. Sometimes, when the light fell just right across the window, the old woman would smile the same young, sad, impossibly tender smile Mira had seen only once before.
