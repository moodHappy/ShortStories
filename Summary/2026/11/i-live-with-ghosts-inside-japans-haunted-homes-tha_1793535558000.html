“I Live with Ghosts”: Inside Japan’s Haunted Homes That Attract Bargain Hunters

发布时间：2026/11/1 20:19:18

“我与鬼魂同住”：走进吸引人们捡便宜的日本“闹鬼”住宅

Ryua knew there was something strange about her Tokyo rental apartment from the moment she first stepped inside. She was convinced it was haunted.

“When I went to view the apartment, there was a man standing there, staring at me,” she said. “The estate agent couldn't see him, so I realised he must have been a ghost.” A more timid person might have turned around and never come back. Ryua, however, decided to move in anyway.

“I've always been a bit contrary, so I decided I wanted to become friends with the man who was staring at me,” she added.

琉亚（Ryua）第一次走进自己在东京租住的公寓时，就觉得那里不太对劲。她确信，这套房子闹鬼。

“我去看房子的时候，有个男人站在那里，直勾勾地盯着我，”她说，“因为房产中介根本看不到他，我才意识到，他一定是个鬼魂。”胆子比较小的人，可能当场转身离开，再也不会回来。但琉亚却决定照样搬进去。

“我这个人有点喜欢跟人唱反调，所以我决定和那个盯着我的男鬼交朋友。”她补充道。

Ryua is an idol performer who prefers not to reveal her surname.

琉亚是一名偶像艺人（aidoru），她不愿透露自己的姓氏。

Four years later, she is still living in the same apartment and says that the paranormal activity has never stopped. She regularly hears footsteps and what she describes as ghosts talking.

四年过去了，她仍然住在同一间公寓里，并表示灵异现象至今没有停止。她经常听到脚步声，还有她所说的鬼魂交谈声。

The apartment's previous occupant died by suicide there.

这套公寓的上一任住户，曾在这里结束了自己的生命。

Although Halloween has become increasingly popular in Japan in recent years, many Japanese people take the existence of ghosts seriously throughout the year.

尽管近年来万圣节在日本越来越流行，但对许多日本人来说，鬼魂并不只是在万圣节才值得认真对待的话题。

In Japan, the word “yūrei” refers to restless spirits believed to linger in the world of the living. They are often associated with people who died violent or tragic deaths — including murder, suicide, or dying alone after a life of isolation.

在日本，有一个专门的词叫“幽灵”（yūrei），指的是那些徘徊在人世间、无法安息的灵魂。人们通常认为，这些鬼魂生前经历过暴力或悲剧，例如被谋杀、自杀，或者生前孤独、最终独自死去的人。

If someone dies inside a home, people may believe that their spirit remains there. For house hunters who are not superstitious or simply have no fear of ghosts, however, this can mean one thing: a property at a bargain price.

如果有人死在家中，人们可能会相信，死者的灵魂会继续留在那里。但对于不迷信或根本不怕鬼的购房者、租房者来说，这反而意味着一个机会：他们或许能以非常便宜的价格找到房子。

There is an entire category of Japanese real estate known as “jiko bukken”, a term used for properties associated with deaths or other troubling incidents. They are often described as “stigmatised properties” or “problem properties”.

日本房地产市场有一类专门的房产，被称为“事故物件”（jiko bukken），指的是曾发生过死亡或其他令人不安事件的房屋。这类房产通常被称为“有污点的房产”或“问题房产”。

Under guidelines issued by the Japanese government in 2021, estate agents must disclose certain deaths that have occurred in a property, including suicides, murders, fire-related deaths and cases in which a body was left undiscovered for an extended period. For rental properties, such information generally has to be disclosed for three years. For properties being sold, there is no time limit on the disclosure requirement under the guidelines.

根据日本政府2021年发布的相关指引，房产中介必须披露房屋内发生过的某些死亡事件，包括自杀、谋杀、火灾导致的死亡，以及尸体长时间未被发现的情况。对于出租房屋，这类信息通常需要披露三年；对于出售的房产，相关指引没有设定披露期限。

The rules are different elsewhere. In Britain, there is generally no legal requirement to disclose that someone has died in a property. In the United States, requirements vary by state. California, for example, requires disclosure of deaths that occurred within the previous three years, while Arizona and Oregon generally do not require such disclosure.

其他国家和地区的规定则有所不同。在英国，法律通常没有要求必须披露有人曾在房屋内死亡。在美国，相关规定因州而异。例如，加利福尼亚州要求披露过去三年内发生在房屋中的死亡事件，而亚利桑那州和俄勒冈州通常没有这项要求。

When Ryua rented her apartment, she was told that a man had died by suicide there. That disclosure helped her secure a discount of nearly 30% on a small, 25-square-metre studio in the fashionable Tokyo neighbourhood of Setagaya.

琉亚租下这套公寓时，中介告诉她，曾有一名男子在里面自杀。正因为这段经历，她才能以接近七折的价格，租下东京时尚住宅区世田谷区一套面积仅25平方米的单身公寓。

She says she pays 45,000 yen a month — around $285 or £213 — instead of the usual 62,000 yen.

她说，自己每月只需支付4.5万日圆，约合285美元或213英镑，而这套公寓原本的月租为6.2万日圆。

Homes where murders or suicides have occurred can be difficult to sell or rent in many countries. But Erik Oskamp, a Tokyo-based estate agent, says Japanese people are particularly superstitious and often strongly avoid properties associated with death.

无论在哪个国家，发生过谋杀或自杀事件的房屋，通常都更难出售或出租。但在东京从事房地产中介工作的埃里克·奥斯坎普（Erik Oskamp）表示，日本人尤其忌讳这类事情，对发生过死亡事件的房产往往避之不及。

“Even an apartment next to a cemetery can be difficult to rent, especially if you can see the gravestones from the kitchen,” he explained.

“即使只是墓地旁边的公寓，也会很难出租，尤其是如果你从厨房就能看到墓碑的话。”他解释道。

“The worst cases are suicides inside the apartment. Even if someone jumps from the roof, many tenants will move out. Other issues include serious crimes and people dying alone.”

“最严重的情况是在公寓内部发生自杀。即使有人是从屋顶跳下去的，也有很多租客会选择搬走。其他影响房屋出租的问题还包括严重犯罪，以及独自死在家中的情况。”

Dying alone, sometimes referred to in Japan as “kodokushi”, does not usually have the same impact on property prices in Britain or the United States. But in the countryside of Kagoshima, in southwestern Japan, it helped Diane Tincher, an American woman, buy a spacious three-bedroom house for about half its usual market price.

在日本，这种独自死在家中的现象有时被称为“孤独死”（kodokushi）。在英国或美国，这类情况通常不会对房价造成同样明显的影响。但在日本西南部鹿儿岛的乡村地区，一栋曾发生孤独死事件的房子，却让一位美国女性黛安·廷彻（Diane Tincher）以大约半价买下了一套宽敞的三居室住宅。

“If there really is a ghost, I thank her.”

“如果那里真的有鬼魂，我反而要感谢她。”

The 79-year-old American has lived in Japan for nearly 40 years and works as a tour guide. She says the estate agent was completely upfront with her: the property had been stigmatized because an elderly woman had died there alone, and that was why it was being sold at such a substantial discount.

这位79岁的美国女性已经在日本生活了近40年，目前从事导游工作。她说，房产中介向她坦诚说明了房子的情况：一位老妇人曾在这里孤独死去，因此这套房产被视为有污点的房屋，售价也因此大幅降低。

Tincher bought the house for 20 million yen, while similar properties in the area can typically sell for as much as 45 million yen.

廷彻最终以2000万日圆买下了这栋房子，而当地类似的房产通常售价可高达4500万日圆。

“I don't believe in ghosts. But I know Japanese people are very superstitious,” she said. “If there really is a ghost, I thank her every day for allowing me to live in such a beautiful and affordable home.”

“我不相信鬼魂的存在，但我知道日本人非常迷信，”她说，“如果那里真的有鬼魂，我每天都会感谢她……让我能够住进这么漂亮、价格又如此实惠的房子。”

Tincher says she effectively received a 50% discount on her home in the Japanese countryside.

廷彻表示，自己在日本乡村买下的这套房子，实际上享受了约五折的优惠。

While researching the property, Tincher came across a website called “Oshimaland”.

在调查这套房产的背景时，廷彻发现了一个名为“Oshimaland”的网站。

The site lists properties associated with deaths and other incidents, along with details of what happened. It was launched in 2005, initially covering properties in Tokyo before expanding across Japan. In 2011, it also began including properties outside the country.

这个网站专门列出发生过死亡事件或其他特殊事件的房产，并提供相关事件的具体信息。它于2005年上线，最初只收录东京地区的房产，后来逐渐扩展到日本全国，并于2011年开始收录日本以外的房产。

Oshimaland is a crowdsourced platform, meaning that members of the public can submit properties and add information about their histories.

Oshimaland是一个众包平台，也就是说，普通公众可以自行提交房产信息，并补充这些房屋过去发生过的事情。

Its founder, Teru Oshima, explains that such properties were once “not a very attractive option” in Japan.

网站创办人大岛照（Teru Oshima）解释说，过去在日本，这类房产“并不是一个很有吸引力的选择”。

But attitudes may be changing. With Japanese property prices rising in recent years and the overall cost of living increasing significantly, a 2025 survey found that more than half of Japanese respondents would now consider renting a stigmatized property if the price were sufficiently low.

不过，这种观念可能正在发生变化。近年来，日本房价不断上涨，整体生活成本也大幅增加。2025年的一项调查发现，如果价格足够便宜，如今超过半数的日本受访者愿意考虑租住事故物件。

Back in Setagaya, Ryua says she remains perfectly happy living alongside her ghostly roommate.

回到世田谷区，琉亚表示，自己仍然很享受与那位鬼魂室友共同生活的日子。

“I believe ghosts were once living people, so they deserve our respect,” she said.

“我相信鬼魂曾经也是活生生的人，所以他们值得我们尊重。”她说。
